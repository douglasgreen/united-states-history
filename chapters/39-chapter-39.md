## Outline from README

- **Question and claim:** How did movements force enforceable rights, and what remained beyond those rights? Local organization, national campaigns, federal action, economic demands, and competing strategies changed one another.
- **Sections and coverage:** §1 Sit-ins and SNCC; §2 Freedom Rides and Albany; §3 Birmingham and Washington; §4 Mississippi and Freedom Summer; §5 Selma and voting rights; §6 Black Power, urban rebellions, and 1968; §7 The Debate. The 1954 starting point is framing context, not a duplicate account of Chapter 38.
- **People and places:** Ella Baker, Fannie Lou Hamer, John Lewis, Diane Nash, Martin Luther King Jr., Malcolm X, Stokely Carmichael/Kwame Ture; Greensboro, Birmingham, Jackson, Selma, Detroit, and Memphis.
- **Opening / In Focus:** Greensboro on February 1, 1960 / a verified Louisiana literacy test and its administration, not an assumption that every state used the same test.
- **Debate:** Nonviolence, federal partnership and surveillance, local versus national leadership, the long movement, and the relation between legal victories and economic justice.
- **Ownership and hand-off:** Own civil/voting-rights legislation’s movement history; Chapter 40 owns the wider legislative system. Alcatraz, post-1968 Chicano/Asian American organizing, and disability rights continue in Chapters 42–43 rather than being backdated. Hand off to Medicare’s signing at the Truman Library.
